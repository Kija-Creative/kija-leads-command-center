# Consumer statistics for the no-website pitch

Synthesized 2026-09-28 from the consumer research topic and its fact check, plus four Google Business Profile findings from the lead-costs topic. Every stat below has a fact checker verdict of confirmed or corrected. The machine copy lives in `research/benchmarks.json` under `consumerStats`.

"Safe to show an owner" means: a small business owner would find it credible, it is 2020 or later (or clearly labelled with its year), and it can be said honestly in one sentence. "Yes, attribute" means the source name and year must appear in the same sentence. "No" means keep it for internal reasoning only.

## Wording rules for generated sentences

1. Name the source and year inline: "BrightLocal's 2026 survey of 1,002 US consumers found..."
2. Use "said" and "about". Never turn a survey share into a promise about this business's revenue.
3. Never state a national percentage of businesses without a website as fact (section 7).
4. The BrightLocal Consumer Search Behavior 2026 series surveyed 1,227 US consumers who had searched for a local business in the last three months. Describe them as "recent local searchers", not "all consumers". Only the 84% figure speaks to the general population.
5. Vendor surveys (DreamHost, GoDaddy) are labelled as such: "a 2025 survey by web host DreamHost".
6. Show the star-threshold stat only when the lead's verified rating is 4.5 or higher.

## Do not use

Never use these in a pitch, even if a researcher pastes them in.

**Verified as untraceable or misattributed (fact checker confirmed the block):**

- **dnu-46pct-local-intent**: "46% of all Google searches have local intent". Only origin is a 2018 tweet about a verbal remark at a Google event ([Search Engine Roundtable](https://www.seroundtable.com/google-46-of-searches-have-local-intent-26529.html)). Google never published it.
- **dnu-google-ipsos-gbp**: "complete Google profiles are 2.7x more likely to be considered reputable, 70% more likely to attract visits, 50% more likely to lead to purchase". Credited to Google/Ipsos Connect only in a 2019 co-branded Sprout Social guide, with no public study. About profiles, not websites.
- **dnu-godaddy-59pct**: "59% (or 60%) of small businesses have no website (GoDaddy)". A 2015 global survey of 1 to 5 person businesses in 8 countries, not a US figure.

**Not traced to any primary source (researcher blocklist):**

- "75% of consumers judge a company's credibility by its website design" (attributed to Stanford; not on the Stanford Guidelines for Web Credibility page).
- "88% of consumers who do a local search on a smartphone call or visit within 24 hours".
- "62% of customers will ignore a business without a web presence" (Network Solutions blog).
- "Businesses without a website lose up to 80% of customers".
- "27% of small businesses have no website in 2025" (vendor blogs).
- "50% might not buy from a business without a website" (Time Warner Cable Business Class, 2015, primary not found).
- "More than half of US small businesses do not have a website" (Verisign e-book asserts it through a link with no survey figure).
- DreamHost's "websites deliver a 41% trust lift" (derivation not explained).
- "60 to 70% of ChatGPT local results come from Foursquare" (BrightLocal only says "reports suggest").
- Any "Kapost and CXL 2026 study" or similar AI roundup stats, and any quiz vendor conversion benchmark.

## Gaps

- No government source (Census ABS or BTOS, SBA) measures website ownership.
- Clutch's full 2025 report was blocked, so its methodology is unverified.
- No independent study measures the direct revenue effect of having no website. The ROI math rests on ticket and margin benchmarks (`benchmarks.md`), not on a consumer stat.
